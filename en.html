My Story

I’ve always had the feeling that somewhere, nature had hidden a cheat sheet to the human psyche. So I set out to find a way to read people faster than they can say, “Well, actually, I’m a very sociable person…”

The hardest part wasn’t even learning to read faces. It was putting the word “physiognomist” in my professional profile. Because for an academic psychologist, it sounds about as unconventional as a neuroscience professor suddenly announcing, “And now, let’s talk about karma.”

Focusing on HR, personnel assessment, and organizational research, and I kept coming back to one question: Is there a way to understand who you’re dealing with more quickly?

Not an hour-and-a-half interview. Not endless tests. But within just a few minutes. That’s how I ended up falling into physiognomy.

First came the books. Then in-person training. Then more books. Then more training. Because my inner skeptic was always grumbling: “Don’t believe it. Test it.”

For me, physiognomy is a fast-track way of getting to know a person. You look at someone’s face, and you can already begin to form an understanding of their thinking style, communication style, response to stress, strengths, and vulnerabilities.

I still question, compare, analyze, and look for neurophysiological explanations behind what I observe, because I feel most at home where there is room for questions, doubts, and exploration.

That’s why I’m here: to introduce you to evidence-based physiognomy. No fortune-telling or mysticism — just critical thinking and a genuine curiosity about how much our own face can reveal about us.
